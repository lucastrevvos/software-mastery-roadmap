DAY 02 · 2026-09-24

Build the verb correctly before adding vocabulary

from real conversation

Today's pattern: most errors came from choosing the right words but building the verb phrase with the wrong structure. The priority is to make the core sentence automatic.

ORIGINAL

Why you stated in JS?

NATURAL

Why did you start with JavaScript?

Past questions: question word + did + subject + base verb. After did, use start, not started.
ORIGINAL

Where you from?

NATURAL

Where are you from?

Questions with BE: move am/is/are before the subject. No do/does/did is needed.
ORIGINAL

If you want I can to show you.

NATURAL

If you want, I can show you.

Modal verbs: after can, could, will, would, should, must, use the base verb without to: can show, will help, should study.
ORIGINAL

She like to help the people. / She's need to use...

NATURAL

She likes helping people. / She needs to use it...

Third person: in the simple present, he/she/it usually takes -s: likes, needs, loses.
ORIGINAL

Know PHP help me a lot.

NATURAL

Knowing PHP helped me a lot.

Verb as subject: when an action is the subject of the sentence, the -ing form is often natural: Knowing PHP helped..., Learning JavaScript takes time.
ORIGINAL

I felt that I can't do nothing without Google.

NATURAL

I felt like I couldn't do anything without Google.

Tense + negatives: keep the past frame consistent: felt → couldn't. In standard English, prefer couldn't do anything instead of a double negative.
ORIGINAL

I will to sleep now.

NATURAL

I'm going to sleep now.

Natural intention: for an immediate plan, I'm going to + verb is natural. If you use will, it is I will sleep, never I will to sleep.

Recall

  1. Complete: “Why ___ you start learning C#?”
  2. Complete: “Where ___ she from?”
  3. Choose: “I can to explain” or “I can explain”?
  4. Complete: “She ___ Python for her research.”
  5. Complete: “Learning SQL ___ me understand databases better.”

Repair

Fix these without looking at the rules:

  1. What you are learning now?
  2. I can to build it.
  3. She need Python.
  4. Know JavaScript help me with Node.js.
  5. I realized that I can't get a job.

Transfer

Answer naturally in English:

  1. Why did you decide to rebuild your fundamentals?
  2. What are you studying right now?
  3. How does knowing PHP help you with JavaScript?
  4. What does your girlfriend need Python for?

Retest target

Tomorrow, hold a short conversation and watch for these four automatic patterns:

  1. did + base verb
  2. be + subject in questions
  3. modal + base verb
  4. she/he/it + verb-s

DAY 01 · 2026-09-23

Questions, verb forms & natural phrasing

from today's chat
ORIGINAL

Who never did it.

NATURAL

Who hasn't done that?

Pattern: use the present perfect for life experience up to now: have/has + past participle.
ORIGINAL

I was to think in add a English teacher...

NATURAL

I was thinking about adding an English teacher...

Patterns: was/were + verb-ing for an ongoing thought; after about, use a gerund: about adding; use an before a vowel sound: an English teacher.
ORIGINAL

I did a lot mistakes in programming.

NATURAL

I've made a lot of mistakes in programming.

Collocation: English uses make a mistake, not do a mistake. Also remember a lot of + noun.
ORIGINAL

Do you working with what now Ethan?

NATURAL

What are you working with now, Ethan?

Question structure: with the present continuous, move be before the subject: What + are + you + working with?. Do not add do.
ORIGINAL IDEA

delete without where is classic

NATURAL PROGRAMMING ENGLISH

Deleting without a WHERE clause is a classic and unforgettable mistake.

Recall

  1. Complete: “What ___ you working on right now?”
  2. Complete: “I've ___ many mistakes while learning.”
  3. Choose: “thinking about add” or “thinking about adding”?
  4. Complete: “Who ___ done that before?”

Transfer

Write these naturally in English without translating word by word:

  1. Com que stack você está trabalhando agora?
  2. Eu já cometi muitos erros programando.
  3. Eu estava pensando em adicionar outra pessoa ao grupo.
  4. Quem nunca fez isso?
