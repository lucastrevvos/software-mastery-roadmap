DAY 11 · 2026-10-04

Retest: she needs, but does she need?

no-new-evidence retest

No reliable new spontaneous English was available after the previous review. Rather than repeat the same auxiliary drill again or invent a weakness, today's lesson returns to another unresolved Error Ledger pattern: third-person -s in statements, and removing that -s when does carries the agreement.

ANCHORS FROM LUCAS'S SPEECH

She like to help the people. / She's need to use...

NATURAL VERSIONS

She likes helping people. / She needs to use it.

Decision rule: in the simple present, he/she/it normally gives the main verb -s: she needs, he works. But after does/doesn't, the auxiliary carries that grammar, so the main verb returns to base form: Does she need...?, She doesn't need....

Recall

  1. She ___ Python for her research. (need/needs)
  2. Does she ___ Python every day? (use/uses)
  3. The worker ___ messages from RabbitMQ. (consume/consumes)
  4. Does the API ___ authentication? (require/requires)

Repair

  1. She need Python for her research.
  2. Does she needs help with the script?
  3. The service process the file every morning.
  4. Does the worker consumes the message?

Transfer

  1. Say one thing Laís does regularly.
  2. Ask whether she needs Python for a task.
  3. Describe what one backend service does.
  4. Ask whether that service uses a queue.

Retest

Without looking back, produce four original sentences: she + verb-s, does she + base verb, service + verb-s, and does the service + base verb. Keep third-person agreement ACTIVE until both statement and question forms appear correctly in spontaneous conversation.

DAY 10 · 2026-10-03

Retest: switch correctly between be and do/did

no-new-evidence retest

No reliable new spontaneous English was available after the previous review. Instead of inventing a new weakness, today's lesson retests the unresolved auxiliary pattern from a slightly harder angle: choosing between direct be questions and do/did questions.

ANCHORS FROM LUCAS'S SPEECH

Where you from? / I dont knew that interview hat techniques questions...

NATURAL VERSIONS

Where are you from? / I didn't know the interview would have technical questions.

Decision rule: if the main verb is be, move it before the subject: Where are you from?, Was the interview difficult?. With most other verbs, use do/does/did + subject + base verb: What did they ask?, Does the service need Redis?.

Recall

  1. ___ the interview technical? (Was/Did)
  2. What ___ the recruiter ask? (was/did)
  3. Where ___ the client from? (is/does)
  4. Does this service ___ a queue? (need/needs)

Repair

  1. Where the company is from?
  2. What the interviewer asked you?
  3. Did the interview was difficult?
  4. Does the API needs authentication?

Transfer

  1. Ask whether your last interview was difficult.
  2. Ask what the recruiter asked about .NET.
  3. Ask where a hypothetical client is from.
  4. Ask whether an event-driven service needs idempotency.

Retest

Without looking back, create four questions: one with is/are, one with was/were, one with did + base verb, and one with does + base verb. The auxiliary/question pattern stays ACTIVE until it appears correctly in spontaneous conversation.
